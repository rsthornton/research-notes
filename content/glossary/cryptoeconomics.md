# Cryptoeconomics

The study of economic coordination through cryptographic protocols — how [[glossary/system|systems]] achieve consensus and maintain integrity without centralized control.

## Domain Definition

Cryptoeconomics analyzes blockchain networks as complex adaptive [[glossary/system|systems]] where cryptographic mechanisms and economic incentives co-evolve to produce emergent coordination. The field examines how [[glossary/boundary|boundaries]] are maintained through consensus rules, how [[glossary/feedback-loop|feedback loops]] govern token economies, and how [[glossary/emergence|emergent]] properties like trust and security arise from mechanism design.

**Systems Framing**:

A blockchain is a distributed [[glossary/system|system]] whose:
- **[[glossary/boundary|Boundary]]** is defined by consensus rules (what transactions are valid)
- **[[glossary/component|Components]]** include nodes, validators, smart contracts, and users
- **[[glossary/state|State]]** is the ledger — a complete record of all valid transactions
- **[[glossary/flow|Flows]]** include tokens, messages, and cryptographic proofs
- **[[glossary/feedback-loop|Feedback]]** operates through staking rewards, slashing penalties, and fee markets

The central challenge is achieving coordination without centralized control — creating [[glossary/emergence|emergent]] order from distributed [[glossary/component|components]] following local rules.

## Key Concepts

Core terms in cryptoeconomics, each grounded in systems thinking:

- [[glossary/consensus-mechanism|Consensus Mechanism]] — how distributed components agree on state
- [[glossary/tokenomics|Tokenomics]] — the economic flows that incentivize behavior
- [[glossary/mechanism-design|Mechanism Design]] — engineering incentive structures
- [[glossary/smart-contract|Smart Contract]] — autonomous subsystems executing on-chain
- [[glossary/protocol|Protocol]] — the rules defining system boundaries and valid operations

## Research Context

This domain draws from research in distributed systems, game theory, and institutional economics. Key sources include work on mechanism design, Byzantine fault tolerance, and the economics of blockchain protocols.
