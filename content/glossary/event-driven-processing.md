# Event-Driven Processing

Computation triggered by events ([[glossary/spike|spikes]]) rather than synchronized clock cycles — the processing paradigm of [[glossary/neuromorphics|neuromorphic]] systems.

In conventional computing, all circuits update every clock tick regardless of whether data changed. In event-driven systems, [[glossary/component|components]] are dormant until they receive input. This asynchronous approach dramatically reduces power consumption for sparse, temporally structured data.

## Systems Connection

Event-driven processing exemplifies how [[glossary/structure|structure]] enables [[glossary/function|function]]. By organizing computation around discrete [[glossary/flow|flows]] ([[glossary/spike|spikes]]) rather than continuous clock signals, [[glossary/neuromorphic-hardware|neuromorphic systems]] achieve energy efficiency that [[glossary/emergence|emerges]] from their architecture rather than their algorithms.

## Key Benefits

- **Power efficiency** — idle circuits consume no energy
- **Low latency** — no waiting for clock cycles
- **Natural fit** — matches temporal structure of real-world data
