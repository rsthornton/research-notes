# Nation-State

The [[glossary/institution|institutional]] [[glossary/system|system]] with a monopoly on legitimate coercion within a territory — the dominant form of [[glossary/governance|governance]] organization.

Nation-states provide public goods (defense, law enforcement, infrastructure), define and enforce [[glossary/property-rights|property rights]], and coordinate [[glossary/collective-action|collective action]] at scale. They are complex [[glossary/system|systems]] with executive, legislative, judicial, and administrative [[glossary/subsystem|subsystems]].

## Systems Connection

The nation-state is a [[glossary/system|system]] with defined [[glossary/boundary|boundaries]] (territory, citizenship), [[glossary/structure|structure]] (branches, agencies, levels), and [[glossary/function|function]] ([[glossary/governance|governance]]). It maintains itself through [[glossary/feedback-loop|feedback loops]] of taxation, service provision, and legitimacy. [[glossary/adaptation|Adaptation]] occurs through political processes — elections, reforms, revolutions.

## Key Tensions

- **Efficiency vs. accountability** — centralization trades off with responsiveness
- **Order vs. liberty** — security requires constraint
- **Scale vs. local knowledge** — states aggregate but may lose information

## Disambiguation

Not to be confused with [[glossary/state|state (systems)]] — the configuration of a system at a moment. The nation-state is a type of institutional system; "state" in systems science refers to variable values.
