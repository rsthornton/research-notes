# Commons

Shared resources that are rivalrous but non-excludable — where one person's use diminishes availability for others, yet access cannot easily be restricted.

Classic examples: fisheries, pastures, aquifers, atmosphere. The "tragedy of the commons" (Hardin) describes overexploitation when individual incentives conflict with collective sustainability. Ostrom showed commons can be sustainably managed through appropriate [[glossary/institution|institutions]].

## Systems Connection

A commons is a [[glossary/system|system]] where [[glossary/component|components]] (users) draw from shared [[glossary/state|state]] (the resource stock). Without [[glossary/governance|governance]], [[glossary/feedback-loop|feedback]] is delayed — individual benefit is immediate while collective cost is distributed. Sustainable commons management requires institutional [[glossary/structure|structures]] that internalize externalities.

## Ostrom's Design Principles

- Clear [[glossary/boundary|boundaries]] — who can access
- Proportional [[glossary/feedback-loop|feedback]] — rules match local conditions
- [[glossary/polycentric-governance|Polycentric]] monitoring and enforcement
