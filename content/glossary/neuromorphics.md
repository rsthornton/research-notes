# Neuromorphics

Brain-inspired computing architectures that process information through spike-based dynamics — [[glossary/system|systems]] designed to emulate the [[glossary/structure|structure]] and [[glossary/function|function]] of biological neural networks.

## Domain Definition

Neuromorphic computing studies how to build artificial [[glossary/system|systems]] that capture the computational principles of biological brains. Unlike conventional computers that separate memory and processing, neuromorphic architectures integrate computation and memory at the [[glossary/component|component]] level — mirroring how neurons and synapses co-locate storage and processing.

**Systems Framing**:

A neuromorphic system is characterized by:
- **[[glossary/structure|Structure]]** — massively parallel, locally connected, hierarchically organized
- **[[glossary/component|Components]]** — artificial neurons and synapses (often memristors)
- **[[glossary/flow|Flows]]** — discrete spikes rather than continuous signals
- **[[glossary/state|State]]** — distributed across synaptic weights and membrane potentials
- **[[glossary/adaptation|Adaptation]]** — learning through spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP)

The key insight is that biological brains achieve remarkable efficiency through their [[glossary/structure|structural]] organization: event-driven processing, temporal coding, and local learning rules create [[glossary/emergence|emergent]] intelligence from simple [[glossary/component|components]].

## Key Concepts

Core terms in neuromorphic computing, each grounded in systems thinking:

- [[glossary/spiking-neural-network|Spiking Neural Network]] — systems processing information through discrete temporal events
- [[glossary/memristor|Memristor]] — components whose state depends on history
- [[glossary/neuromorphic-hardware|Neuromorphic Hardware]] — physical substrates for brain-inspired computation
- [[glossary/temporal-coding|Temporal Coding]] — information encoded in spike timing
- [[glossary/synaptic-plasticity|Synaptic Plasticity]] — adaptive modification of connection strengths

## Research Context

This domain bridges neuroscience, computer engineering, and materials science. Key research areas include memristive devices, spiking neural network algorithms, and neuromorphic chip architectures like Intel's Loihi and IBM's TrueNorth.
